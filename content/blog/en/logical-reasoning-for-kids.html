Logical reasoning is the ability to observe, find rules and draw conclusions from them. It's behind math, reading a word problem, programming and many everyday decisions. The good news is that it can be trained like any other skill, with activities that children usually find fun.

What the curriculum says

In Brazil's national curriculum (BNCC) for Mathematics, logical reasoning appears from Grade 1: sorting objects by color, shape and size (EF01MA09), finding what's missing in sequences (EF01MA10) and describing repeating and recursive patterns (EF02MA10). Later come equalities, the foundation of algebra. The BNCC's Computing supplement adds computational thinking: recognizing patterns, working with true and false and creating algorithms, which are sequences of steps to solve something.

7 activities, from the simplest to the most challenging

1. Repeating patterns

Make a row of objects or drawings and ask the child to continue it: 🔴 🔵 🔵 🔴 🔵 🔵 🔴 … What comes next? (🔵). Then swap roles: let the child create the pattern and you continue it. Explaining the rule ("one red, two blue") is just as important as getting the next piece right.

2. Number sequences

Start with addition rules and build up:

  • 2, 5, 8, 11, … (add 3 each time: next is 14)
  • 50, 45, 40, 35, … (take away 5: next is 30)
  • 1, 2, 4, 7, 11, … (add 1, then 2, then 3, then 4: next is 16)

The last one is a recursive sequence: each term depends on the one before, and the "rule" also changes. It's a great challenge for Grades 3 and 4.

3. Which one is different?

Show four items and ask which one doesn't go with the others, and why: 🍎 🍌 🥕 🍇 (the carrot, because it isn't a fruit), or 4, 8, 15, 20 (15, because it's odd).

More than one answer can be right. If the child gives a good reason for a different choice from yours, the answer is right. The goal is to train observation and justification, not to guess what the adult was thinking.

4. Greater, less or equal without doing the whole sum

Ask whether 3 + 5 is greater than, less than or equal to 4 + 4. Or whether 19 + 7 is greater than 20 + 7. The idea is for the child to compare by looking at the structure ("19 is less than 20, and both add 7") instead of calculating everything. This builds number sense.

5. Balance scales

Draw a balanced scale. If 2 apples weigh the same as 1 banana, and 2 bananas weigh the same as 1 watermelon, how many apples balance the watermelon? (4.) Scales show that an equation stays true when you do the same thing to both sides, the same idea that, years later, solves equations.

6. Symbol puzzles

Replace unknown numbers with symbols:

  • ★ + ★ = 10
  • ★ + ● = 8

What is ● worth? First the child finds out that ★ is 5 and then that ● is 3. It's algebra without letters: finding the unknown value that makes the equation true (BNCC skill EF04MA15).

7. Step-by-step instructions

One person is the "robot" and can only follow simple commands: "take 3 steps forward", "turn right", "repeat 2 times". The child writes the instructions for the robot to reach an object in the room. If the robot goes the wrong way, they look for the mistake in the instructions and fix it. It's programming without a computer: sequence, repetition and debugging.

How to get the most out of these activities

  • Ask "how did you figure it out?". Saying the reasoning out loud makes it clearer, for the child and for you.
  • Don't give away the answer. Give a smaller hint: "look at the difference between the first and second number".
  • Let the child create challenges for you. Inventing a sequence requires understanding the rule very well.
  • Increase the difficulty gradually. If they get three wrong in a row, take a step back.

Logical reasoning in Educa Game

Educa Game has a whole Logic trail, separate from Math. At the Beginner level there are patterns, sequences, "which one is different?" and greater or less. At Intermediate come 4×4 Sudoku, magic squares, balance scales and true or false. Then come propositions, sets, detective puzzles, truth tables, the classic knights and knaves puzzle and step-by-step programming. Each skill shows how it relates to the BNCC, and the game adjusts the difficulty to each child's performance.
