Math in Brazil's BNCC, Grades 1 to 5: what children learn each year

By Ricardo Georgel · November 21, 2026 · 4 min read

The BNCC (Base Nacional Comum Curricular) is Brazil's national curriculum: it defines what every student in the country should learn at each stage of school. For parents, it helps to understand what is expected of their child each year. For teachers, it's the reference for planning. Even outside Brazil, it's a clear map of how math builds up year by year. This guide summarizes the Mathematics section for the early years of elementary school, Grades 1 to 5.

How to read a BNCC code

Each skill has a code. For example, EF02MA07:

  • EF: Ensino Fundamental (elementary and middle school);
  • 02: Grade 2;
  • MA: Mathematics;
  • 07: the number of the skill within that grade.

So EF02MA07 is the 7th Mathematics skill of Grade 2, which deals with multiplication as repeated addition. When a report or a material says "works on EF04MA15", just read the code to know the grade and the subject.

The five thematic units

Mathematics in the BNCC is organized into five units that appear in every grade, with increasing complexity:

  1. Numbers: counting, the decimal system, operations, fractions and decimals.
  2. Algebra: patterns, sequences and equalities. In the early grades, it's the preparation for equations.
  3. Geometry: shapes, location and movement.
  4. Quantities and measurement: length, mass, capacity, time, money, perimeter and area.
  5. Probability and statistics: chance, tables and graphs.

What is expected each year

Below are some highlights from each grade, with the codes. The summaries are short versions, not the official text, which is on the BNCC website, in Portuguese (basenacionalcomum.mec.gov.br).

Grade 1

  • Use numbers to show quantity and order, and count collections of up to 100 objects (EF01MA01, EF01MA04).
  • Compare numbers with up to two digits and compose and decompose tens and ones (EF01MA05, EF01MA07).
  • Build basic addition facts and solve addition and subtraction problems (EF01MA06, EF01MA08).
  • Sort objects by color, shape and size and complete sequences (EF01MA09, EF01MA10).
  • Recognize coins and banknotes of the Brazilian real (EF01MA19).

Grade 2

  • Numbers up to the hundreds, with place value (EF02MA01, EF02MA04).
  • Addition and subtraction with numbers of up to three digits, including mental math (EF02MA05, EF02MA06).
  • First multiplications, by 2, 3, 4 and 5, as repeated addition, and the ideas of double, half, triple and a third (EF02MA07, EF02MA08).
  • Patterns and sequences with a rule (EF02MA09 to EF02MA11).
  • Measure time intervals and make exchanges with money (EF02MA19, EF02MA20).

Grade 3

  • Basic addition and multiplication facts (EF03MA03), the foundation of the times tables.
  • Multiplication by 2, 3, 4, 5 and 10, including in rows and columns (EF03MA07).
  • First divisions, with and without remainders (EF03MA08).
  • Understand equality: different calculations can give the same result (EF03MA11).
  • Tell the time on digital and analog clocks (EF03MA23).

Grade 4

  • Use the properties of operations to calculate in different ways (EF04MA05).
  • Broader multiplication and division, with divisors of up to two digits (EF04MA06, EF04MA07).
  • Unit fractions (1/2, 1/3, 1/4…) and the first decimals, linked to money (EF04MA09, EF04MA10).
  • Find the unknown number in an equation, the seed of algebra (EF04MA14, EF04MA15).
  • Area on grid paper and shopping problems with change (EF04MA21, EF04MA25).

Grade 5

  • Read, write and order decimal numbers (EF05MA02).
  • Fractions as part of a whole, equivalent fractions and comparing fractions and decimals (EF05MA03 to EF05MA05).
  • Simple percentages: 10%, 25%, 50%, 75% and 100% (EF05MA06).
  • Counting problems using the multiplication principle (EF05MA09).
  • Equations with an unknown value (EF05MA10, EF05MA11) and the relationship between perimeter and area (EF05MA20).

How to use the BNCC to help the child

  • For parents: if the child struggles with a topic, see in which grade it first appears. Struggles with fractions in Grade 5 often come from division or place value that was shaky before. It's worth going back a step.
  • For teachers: the codes help you match activities, assignments and reports to the school's plan and explain to families what is being worked on.
  • For everyone: the BNCC says what to learn, not each child's pace. It's normal to be a bit ahead in one topic and behind in another.
